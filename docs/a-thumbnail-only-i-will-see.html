A thumbnail only I will see

9 November 2026

2 min read

I've spent time designing a thumbnail for a Sketch template that probably only I will ever see.

The template goes with a Codex skill I made to generate a style guide from an existing design. Both took a fair amount of work. Once they were finished, I wanted to give the image representing the template in Sketch's file browser some attention too.

I started by importing the Swatches icon from Phosphor Icons as an SVG. Then I played with it. I used a bright blue background, tried different shades of blue, overlapped and offset shapes, and adjusted the opacity of the layers. Repeated outlines gave it some depth. I particularly liked how the shapes looked together as the layers built up.

I can't remember where the original blue came from. Possibly something I'd seen on Dribbble, or something I found while working in Sketch. I hadn't planned a palette. I liked the colour and carried on from there.

Most of my design work is websites and interfaces. I enjoy making those look good, but they also need to make sense to someone using them. People need to recognise things and find their way through a task.

This was a thumbnail. I knew what it was for, which gave me somewhere to start, but there was very little else to worry about. I could spend time adjusting shapes and colours simply because I liked how they looked.

The template would have worked just as well without any of this. But I'd put a lot into the project, and I wanted its thumbnail to look good too.
