Small Systems That Make Life Easier

26 August 2026

2 min read

The systems I find most useful often do one small, repetitive job. Once I have set one up, I no longer have to remember that job myself.

My loft temperature log is a good example. An automation records the temperature four times a day. I don't have to remember to take each reading, and the readings accumulate in the log.

I use the same approach for jobs that don't happen every day. Apple Reminders tells me when to replace toothbrush heads and razor blades, and when to order a prescription. Without a reminder, these jobs are easy to forget until I need to do them.

Every week, an automation generates a review of my blog ideas. I don't have to remember to collect the ideas and review them on a particular day. I still decide which ideas are worth developing into posts.

Project documentation isn't an automation, but organising it properly solves a similar problem. Each piece of information has a place, so I know where to look for it. When code changes, I ask Codex to update the documentation at the same time. This stops the documentation describing an older version of the code.

I also update software before I expect to use it. If I leave an update until I am ready to make something, I have to spend that time waiting for the software instead. Updating it earlier means it is ready when I have the energy to use it.

The rule I use is simple. If a job repeats and doesn't require a new decision each time, I consider setting a reminder, choosing one place for the information or automating it. I keep the decisions about what to write and what to make for myself.

None of these systems is complicated. They record temperature readings, remind me about replacements and prescriptions, collect blog ideas, keep documentation current and prepare software for later. They do the repetitive work while I decide what is worth my attention.
