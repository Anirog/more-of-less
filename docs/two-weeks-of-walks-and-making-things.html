Two Weeks of Walks and Making Things

27 March 2026

3 min read

The past couple of weeks have felt quiet on the outside and fairly busy in my head. I've been trying to get back to simple things that actually help: getting up early, doing yoga and meditation, reading, tidying up, eating something decent, and going for morning walks again. It's all pretty ordinary, but it has been doing me some good.

The walks especially. I'd forgotten that a short walk in the morning can make such a difference to the rest of the day. It's not just the walking, though I do love that feeling of being out and starting to feel awake. It's coming home afterwards that I really like. By the time you get home, you're more awake and feel like the day has got off to a good start.

One morning this week I was up at 5, did yoga and meditation, read for a while, tidied my room, and got out to the shop, all before 10. When I have a morning like that, I feel like a proper person again. Not like a different person, just more like myself.

I've also been reading The Labyrinth of the Spirits by Carlos Ruiz Zafón. It's the fourth book in the Cemetery of Forgotten Books series, and it's exactly the kind of thing I want at the moment. Dark, rich, absorbing. The kind of book that gives me something to enjoy in the quieter parts of the day.

I've also been doing a lot of work on the blog. A lot of it has been small, nerdy, slightly obsessive stuff: refining the HTML, making the structure more semantic, thinking about Jinja templates, keeping the layout minimal, adding a simple back link to posts, figuring out how to preview remote branches locally, and trying to keep the whole thing clean and calm instead of overworked. It probably sounds dry written out like that, but I actually enjoy it. There's something reassuring about chipping away at tiny details until a thing starts to feel right.

The same idea keeps coming up in both the blog and life really: less noise, more clarity. Fewer moving parts. Things put together properly. A page with just a title and a date can feel more honest than something with too much going on. I get more out of a morning walk than I do from making a big plan. A tidy room, a decent breakfast, reading a few pages of a good book, a bit of code that finally makes sense. That's what's been helping me through the week.

None of it has felt particularly exciting, and maybe that's why it feels like real life. I've just been trying to have days that feel a bit easier. Get up. Move a bit. Read something good. Make something small and useful. Come home feeling a little more steady than when I left.
