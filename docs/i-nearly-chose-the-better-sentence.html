I Nearly Chose the Better Sentence

23 August 2026

2 min read

I've been building and repeatedly testing my own Unslop skill, after looking at Poteto's Unslop skill on GitHub and a video about how to install and use it.

The aim is to spot the phrases in AI-written prose that don't sound like something a person would say. During one test, I highlighted those phrases and asked for several natural alternatives instead of accepting a complete rewrite.

Then I nearly chose one because it read better.

It had the rhythm and polish I associate with good writing. But I stopped and realised I had never heard anyone actually say it. I wouldn't say it myself either. It might have been the better sentence, but it wasn't the better sentence for me.

That small choice exposed a problem with AI-assisted writing. Improving prose and preserving someone's voice aren't necessarily the same thing. A rewrite can be clearer, neater and more impressive while moving further away from the person who is supposed to have written it.

The question I found most useful was simple: Would I actually say this?

That doesn't mean writing has to sound exactly like a conversation. It means noticing the difference between a genuine improvement and a flourish that is attractive mainly because it sounds like "good writing".

One change made that difference especially clear. The abstract phrase "durable notes matter more than disposable summaries" became "check whether saved notes are easy to find and remember." The second version does more than sound natural. It tells me what to check.

I've started to think Unslop may work best after AI has produced a draft. That way I can see what it changes, compare the alternatives and decide what still sounds like me. The skill can point out the polished sentence. I still have to decide whether I would ever say it.
